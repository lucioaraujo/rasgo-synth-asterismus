ACID · TONE

An acid bass with a resonant filter, singing the circuit's phrase.

How it works

A bass voice with a resonant low-pass filter and an envelope that opens the filter on every note; the 16-step phrase comes from the seed, and the sky's density and tension open or close the timbre.

The controls

CUT — where the filter cuts: low and muffled on the left, open on the right.

RESO — the filter's resonance: the whistle around the cutoff.

ARTIC — how much the envelope opens the filter on each note: from soft to snappy.

Listen for

Keep CUT low and raise ARTIC: each note opens and closes by itself.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio, the ACID voice; the lineage of resonant-filter basses.

FM FLUTE · TONE

An FM flute, blown phrase by phrase.

How it works

An FM voice with breath: each step of the sky's phrase starts a note, as long as the gesture; the sky's tension and mutations make it tenser.

The controls

BODY — the body of the breath: how far the voice goes into saturation.

BRIGHT — brightness: the FM index — the breath of the attack and the timbre of each note.

PHRASE — the length of each note of the phrase: from staccato to legato.

Listen for

Raise PHRASE: the notes become long lines crossing the other stars.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (FmFluteVoice).

THEREMIN · TONE

A continuous line, gliding from one pitch to the next.

How it works

A tone without attack that chases the body's pitch and glides to it; the score's arc, the intervention and the sky's mutations push it upwards.

The controls

PITCH — the register: how far above or below the body's pitch.

PRES. — presence: the volume and how much the voice saturates.

DRIFT — the vibrato: slower and shallower on the left, faster and deeper on the right.

Listen for

Use it as a thread between percussive stars: it fills the space between notes.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (ThereminVoice).

VOSIM · TONE

A pulsed formant: a voice made of decaying pulses.

How it works

Trains of squared-sine pulses that decay: the repetition gives the pitch, the width of each pulse gives the formant — the VOSIM technique.

The controls

PULSE — the pitch of the pulse.

FORM. — the formant: from a dark vowel to a bright one.

DECAY — the decay of each pulse: from nasal to open.

Listen for

Move only FORM.: the same note with another mouth.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (VosimOscillator); VOSIM, Institute of Sonology, Utrecht (Werner Kaegi; analysis by Stan Tempelaars).

WALSH · TONE

A tone made of Walsh functions: steps, not sines.

How it works

The wave is a sum of Walsh functions (square waves of different orders), so it sounds digital and hollow; the sky's matter and mutations raise its pitch.

The controls

PITCH — the pitch.

SEQUENCE — intensity: louder, rougher.

CUT — raises pitch and roughness together.

Listen for

Compare it with MORPH: both make tones, one by steps, the other by shapes.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (WalshOscillator); functions by Joseph L. Walsh.

HARD SYNC · TONE

One oscillator locked to another: the harmonic tear of sync.

How it works

A slave oscillator is reset on every cycle of the master; the faster the slave, the more torn the timbre — and the sky's tension and TEAR speed it up.

The controls

RATIO — the ratio between slave and master: the timbre.

BODY — the body: level and saturation.

SWEEP — an extra sweep of the slave: more scream.

Listen for

Raise SWEEP slowly: the classic sound of sync opening up.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (HardSyncVoice).

GALO · TONE

A resonant call, now and then.

How it works

A resonant cry that calls on the memory beats — on all of them, one in two or one in four.

The controls

CALL — how many beats call: all, one in two, one in four.

REGISTER — the register of the call.

TAIL — the tail of each call.

Listen for

Keep it rare: one call every four beats marks the form.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (GaloVoice).

TUBA · TONE

A low breath: a tuba of resonances.

How it works

Noisy breath exciting low resonances; the breath follows the piece's arc and the sky's density.

The controls

PITCH — the register: from very low to low.

BREATH — the strength of the breath.

BRIGHT — the brightness of the resonances.

Listen for

It holds the piece from below: try it in place of the BASS.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (OrganismStem::Tuba); local TubaBreathVoice.

FOOTNOTE · TONE

A footnote: a small bell, slightly off, rare.

How it works

A two-partial bell at a ratio that is never clean (1.18 to 1.94, away from the fifth), played on the memory beats.

The controls

PITCH — the pitch: up to two octaves higher.

INHARM. — the ratio between the partials: how off it sounds.

DECAY — the decay: from 0.15 to 1.6 s.

Listen for

Keep it rare and quiet: it comments, it does not lead.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth (FootnotePluck).

HARMONAIG · TONE

A four-voice chord moving in fourths.

How it works

Root, third, fifth and seventh in the sky's mode, moving up a fourth every 1, 2 or 4 beats.

The controls

INVERSION — which tone goes to the bass.

SPREAD — one or two octaves of spread.

MOTION — the chord's pace: every 4, 2 or 1 beat.

Listen for

Put HARMONAIG under a voice: it becomes harmony.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (OrganismStem::Harmonaig); harmonàig by Jason Lim / Instruō.

BLOOM · TONE

A fractal melody: trunk and branch.

How it works

Each beat of the phrase plays the next step of the trunk (5 notes) or of the branch (25); at the end of the turn, the star may switch level.

The controls

PATH — the chance of switching level at the end of the turn.

REGISTER — an octave below, the body's pitch or an octave above.

DURATION — the length of each note.

Listen for

Listen to a whole turn: the branch repeats the trunk in miniature.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (OrganismStem::Bloom).

CORE · TONE

A cross-FM voice on the sky's phrase.

How it works

A pair of oscillators in which the modulator receives the carrier's previous output; each step of the phrase starts a note.

The controls

FM — the modulation index: from pure to metallic.

RATIO — the modulator's ratio: 1 to 3.5, in harmonic ratios.

DECAY — the decay: 0.12 to 1.6 s.

Listen for

Raise FM on a long note: the timbre opens into bells.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (OrganismStem::Core).

BASS · TONE

A phrased bass.

How it works

A softly saturated sine that plays on the memory beats, in one of three octaves.

The controls

REGISTER — the octave: two below, one below or the body's.

ATTACK — the attack: 2 to 80 ms.

DURATION — the length of each note.

Listen for

With a slow ATTACK, the bass breathes instead of hitting.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (OrganismStem::BassFallback).

AY-8910 · TONE

The 8-bit sound chip: three tones, noise and envelope.

How it works

A model of the AY-3-8910: three tones in arpeggio, the register's noise and the hardware envelope in a loop.

The controls

ARPEGGIO — the arpeggio: from the octave to the open triad.

NOISE — the noise: above a third it comes in, and its colour rises.

ENVELOPE — the speed of the looping envelope.

Listen for

Raise ENVELOPE: the old game sound starts to pulse.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (AY8910Chip); General Instrument AY-3-8910.

MORPH · TONE

A tone moving between waveforms.

How it works

Sine, triangle, saw and square in one oscillator; the shape can wander by itself, and each phrase restarts the envelope.

The controls

SHAPE — the waveform.

DRIFT — how much the shape wanders by itself.

DURATION — the length of the envelope: 0.1 to 1.8 s.

Listen for

With DRIFT high, the same note changes colour while it sounds.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (MorphOscillator).

ENV PULSE · TONE

A pulse that repeats by itself.

How it works

A looping envelope striking a tone: rhythm and timbre come from the same loop.

The controls

SPEED — the speed of the loop.

DECAY — the decay of each stroke.

PITCH — the pitch of the tone.

Listen for

With SPEED high, the strokes turn into a new tone.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (EnvelopeLoopPulse).

TURING · TONE

A looping melody that changes little by little.

How it works

The 16-bit register of the "Turing Machine": on each step the outgoing bit comes back or is swapped for a new one; the bits pick the note.

The controls

CHANGE — the chance of a new bit: 0 locks the loop, 0.6 almost never repeats.

SHAPE — the timbre: from sine to square.

DURATION — the tail of each note.

Listen for

Start with CHANGE at zero: hear the loop, then let it change.

Where it comes from

Rewritten from the Rasgo Synth Studio's KSRandomizerCore; the Turing Machine by Tom Whitwell / Music Thing Modular.

MODAL · TONE

A resonating body: memory and resonance.

How it works

A bank of six modes (the partials of a bell or a plate) excited by the sky; the pressure of the relations detunes the modes, memory lengthens the tail. It lives in one body only.

The controls

BODY — the pitch of the resonating body.

BRIGHT — brightness: how much of the high modes comes through.

TAIL — the tail: how long the body takes to fall silent.

Listen for

Set TAIL high and let the other stars play it.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (ModalResonator); the idea of Rings, Émilie Gillet / Mutable Instruments.

GENDYN · TEXTURE

A wave remade on every cycle: stochastic matter.

How it works

The waveform is a line of points that wander at random, in amplitude and in duration, on every turn — Xenakis's dynamic stochastic synthesis. Still, it is almost a tone; stirred, it becomes pitched noise.

The controls

STEP — the size of each point's amplitude step: from a steady tone to a rough one.

TEMPO — the size of the duration step: how much the pitch slides.

FLOW — how hard the signal drives the saturation: more flow, more presence.

Listen for

With STEP and TEMPO near zero, hear the tone being born; raise them slowly until it comes apart.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (GendynOscillator); GENDYN, Iannis Xenakis.

DEJA VU · TEXTURE

Statistical memory: what already sounded comes back, alike.

How it works

The star follows the sky's pitch and density and, on memory beats, plays something that resembles what went by.

The controls

REMEMBER — how strongly the memory imposes itself.

BRIGHT — the brightness of the memory.

DURATION — the length of each return.

Listen for

Listen after a contrast: the DEJA VU brings the beginning back.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (DejaVuVoice).

SPECTRAL · TEXTURE

A spectral object: pitch is rhythm.

How it works

Each partial p sounds at f0·p and pulses p times per period: the same number is pitch and rhythm. Every eight periods, a sieve opens new holes in the spectrum.

The controls

PITCH — the fundamental.

INHARM. — inharmonicity: how far the partials stretch.

PERIOD — the period: 1, 2 or 4 beats.

Listen for

With a long PERIOD, hear the partials light up one by one.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (OrganismStem::Spectral).

THERMAL · TEXTURE

Thermal life: it heats up, dies, comes back.

How it works

A tone that heats up, dies — slowly or suddenly — cools down and returns, with static crackles denser the hotter it gets.

The controls

HEAT — the heat: how high life rises.

DEATH — the chance of dying suddenly.

STATIC — the crackles.

Listen for

With DEATH high, the star becomes an event.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (OrganismStem, thermal life).

TEXTURE · TEXTURE

A bed of coloured noise.

How it works

Noise filtered in a band that moves with the sky's density.

The controls

COLOUR — the colour: from white to pink to brown.

FOCUS — the focus: the resonance of the band.

MOTION — how fast the band moves.

Listen for

Low and dark, it becomes the air of the room.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (OrganismStem::Texture).

RÖSSLER · TEXTURE

A chaotic Rössler attractor, with eruptions.

How it works

The Rössler attractor as a source: a pitched chaotic walk; each eruption of the z axis is an attack.

The controls

RATE — the speed of the walk: the pitch of the chaotic noise.

CHAOS — chaos: from an almost periodic orbit to full chaos.

BRIGHT — brightness: how much of the signal comes through.

Listen for

Raise CHAOS slowly: hear the orbit come apart.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (RosslerMap); attractor by Otto E. Rössler.

GRANULAR · TEXTURE

A cloud of grains from the recent past.

How it works

Short grains read from the last second and a half of a phrase of its own, with a pitch deviation.

The controls

DENSITY — 2 to 40 grains per second.

SPREAD — how far back the grains reach: up to 1.6 s.

PITCH — each grain's pitch deviation: up to 7 semitones.

Listen for

With DENSITY high and SPREAD low, the phrase becomes a mist.

Where it comes from

Rewritten from the Rasgo Synth Studio's GranularCloud; the idea of Clouds, Émilie Gillet / Mutable Instruments.

GHOST · TEXTURE

The spectral ghost of what the sky is playing.

How it works

It listens to the lit stars, keeps the strength of each frequency band and swaps the phase for a drifting one: the harmony stays, without attack or clear pitch.

The controls

DRIFT — from a coherent ghost to a blur.

MEMORY — how long the ghost takes to forget: up to ~0.4 s.

BRIGHT — the cutoff: from dark to open.

Listen for

Put it next to percussive stars: it turns them into a halo.

Where it comes from

Rewritten from the Rasgo Synth Studio's SpectralGhost; the phase randomisation of the Clouds/Beads spectral mode.

VECTOR · TEXTURE

Four timbres on a point that orbits.

How it works

Four corners — stochastic wave, Walsh, Atari Punk and pink noise — blended by the position of a point on a square.

The controls

X — the point's horizontal position.

Y — the point's vertical position.

ORBIT — how much the point circles around, on a slow figure.

Listen for

Set ORBIT to zero and travel the corners with X and Y.

Where it comes from

Rewrite of the Rasgo Synth Studio's vector synthesis; Prophet VS, Chris Meyer / Sequential Circuits.

SCAT · VOICE

Synthetic syllables: a voice improvising without words.

How it works

A formant voice that sings one syllable on each step of the sky's phrase, changing vowel; the sky's memory and mutations stretch the syllables.

The controls

BODY — the body: how heavy the voice is and how much it saturates.

VOWEL — the starting vowel (a, e, i, o, u, which then rotate).

PHRASE — the length of each syllable.

Listen for

Put SCAT next to the DRUMS: the voice answers the groove.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (ScatVoice).

CHOIR · VOICE

A choir of synthetic voices on a minor seventh chord.

How it works

On every phrase, a four-voice chord (root, minor third, fifth, seventh) is sung on a vowel; the register comes from the seed and from BODY.

The controls

BODY — the register and weight of the choir: up to an octave and a half higher.

OPEN. — the opening vowel and how far apart the voices spread.

PHRASE — the length of the phrase and the vowel it ends on.

Listen for

Leave the choir alone with a quiet TEXTURE: the sky becomes a field.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (ChoirEnsemble).

ATROPELO · VOICE

An almost spoken voice, tripping over its syllables.

How it works

Short syllables on the memory beats, with a pitch that rises with the sky's tension and mutations.

The controls

PITCH — the pitch of the speech.

BODY — the body: weight and saturation.

SYLLABLE — the length of each syllable.

Listen for

With a short SYLLABLE and a dense sky, it becomes nervous speech.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (AtropeloVoice).

DRUMS · PULSE

The sky's drum kit: kick, snare, hat and the seed's kit.

How it works

Kick, snare and hat follow the circuit's cells and the polyrhythmic divider; the seed adds cowbell, clap, cymbal and maracas, and in many seeds the DRUMS play groove families that change with each part of the form.

The controls

ACTIV. — activity: more kicks and hits when the sky is dense.

FRAG. — fragmentation: snares and hats off their usual places.

OPEN. — how open the hat is: from closed to open.

Listen for

Mute the other stars and hear the groove change family in the contrast.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (DrumVoices, PolyrhythmDivider, GrooveDialogue as a concept); voices inspired by the TR-808.

ATARI PUNK · PULSE

A stepped pulse from two oscillators: the Atari Punk Console.

How it works

An astable oscillator fires a monostable: out comes a train of stepped pulses that follows the tempo and the sky's time.

The controls

CLOCK — the clock frequency: from beat to buzz.

PULSE — the pulse width: from click to tone.

ATTACK — the attack: level and saturation.

Listen for

With CLOCK low it is rhythm; with CLOCK high, timbre.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (AtariPunkConsole); Stepped Tone Generator by Forrest M. Mims III.

BLUFF · PULSE

Signal, bluff and trust: a call that sometimes lies.

How it works

The star gives signals; only the genuine ones get the answer (a fifth above), weighted by trust — and after a betrayal the answer falls silent until the form reorganises.

The controls

TRUTH — how many of the signals are true.

WINDOW — the window of expectation: 1 to 4 beats.

PITCH — the pitch of the call.

Listen for

Lower TRUTH: hear the answer disappear.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth (BluffSignal).

SIEVE · CONTROL

Control star: Xenakis's sieve on the sky's attacks.

How it works

It makes no sound. It lets through only the attacks that fall in the sieve (A, 0) ∪ (B, R), counted per star.

The controls

MODULUS A — modulus A: 2 to 7.

MODULUS B — modulus B: 3 to 11.

RESIDUE — the residue of B: where the second class starts.

Listen for

Light it in a dense seed: the sky gets holes with a logic.

Where it comes from

Iannis Xenakis's theory of sieves ("Sieves", 1990).

POLYMETER · CONTROL

Control star: each star in its own cycle.

How it works

It makes no sound. Each star counts its own cycle and attacks on only some of the steps; the cycles drift apart.

The controls

CYCLE — the length of the cycle: 3 to 9 steps.

PULSES — how many steps attack: 20 to 90 %.

SPREAD — how much the cycles spread among the stars.

Listen for

With an odd CYCLE and a high SPREAD, the sky becomes polymetric.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (OrganismStem::Polymeter).

HOCKET · CONTROL

Control star: hocket, one attack at a time.

How it works

It makes no sound. The stars take turns: the one that attacked waits for the others.

The controls

GROUP — how many stars share the line: 2 to 4.

PATIENCE — how many times a star waits.

RANGE — the reach: how many stars join the game.

Listen for

Listen with short-attack stars: the melody is split among them.

Where it comes from

Medieval hocket; Rasgo Synth Studio (OrganismStem::Hocket).

RUPTURE · CONTROL

Control star: the sky falls silent all of a sudden.

How it works

It makes no sound. Now and then the whole sky cuts in ~3 ms and comes back in ~20 ms.

The controls

PERIOD — the period: 4 to 16 beats.

DURATION — the length of the silence: 1 to 4 beats.

CHANCE — the chance of each rupture happening.

Listen for

Use a short DURATION: the silence becomes an accent.

Where it comes from

Rasgo Synth Studio (OrganismStem::Rupture).
